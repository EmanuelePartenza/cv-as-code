Dear Harbour Master,

You are looking for someone to run the day-to-day operation of Saltmere and be
the port's first point of contact for the coastguard. That is the part of my
work at Skerra Head that matters most to me: since 2019 I have coordinated
fourteen incidents with the coastguard by VHF, three of them helicopter
evacuations, from a station where I am the only person on the radio.

The rest of the posting reads like the rest of my week. I plan and run the
four supply landings a year — the inventory, the order and the landing plan
agreed with the vessel's master — and manage the station's consumables and
spares budget of about £40,000 a year. I have kept a first-order light, its
fog signal and the standby generator in service as sole keeper, six years
without an unplanned outage. Before Skerra I spent five years on the deck of
the Kirkhaven ferry, six crossings a day, with the radio watch on the bridge
and the GMDSS certificate that came with it.

Reporting is not an afterthought for me. I wrote the station's 40-page
handbook and trained five relief keepers on it, and I built a small telemetry
logger that has recorded fog-signal activations and generator hours for three
years; the Lights Board's engineers now plan their maintenance visits from its
monthly export.

One honest note: I have not worked with a port management system. Berth
planning and vessel scheduling I have done on paper and by radio, for
landings, not for a harbour; I would need to learn your system and I would
expect to learn it quickly, having built my own tools where there were none.

I would be glad to bring the station's habits — the log kept every day, the
radio answered every time — to the port end of the coast.

Yours sincerely,

Robin Ashcombe
