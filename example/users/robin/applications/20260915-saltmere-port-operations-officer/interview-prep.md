# Interview prep — Port Operations Officer, Port of Saltmere Harbour Authority

Internal document for Robin. Not sent to anyone. Strike anything you would not
say. The CV spec for this application is approved (2026-09-18).

## 1. The company and the role (from the posting only)

**What they seem to optimise for.** A small, practical harbour authority that
wants one dependable officer who can run the day without supervision. The
musts are about safety and continuity: marine experience, a radio certificate,
emergency-services coordination, a port management system, written reporting.
Everything "nice" (VTS, traffic monitoring, aids to navigation, French) points
the same way — breadth over specialism.

**What the role owns.**
- Vessel movements and berth allocation across **two basins**.
- The daily port log and a **monthly operations report**.
- First point of contact for the coastguard and emergency services.
- Stores, fuel supplies and contractor visits.
- Aids to navigation, with a maintenance contractor.
- Deputising for the Harbour Master.

**Unknown — clarify in the interview.**
- Which port management system they use, and how much of berth planning is in
  it versus on a whiteboard and the phone.
- Traffic volume: how many movements a day, what mix (ferry, fishing,
  commercial, leisure), and how often the Brumeval ferry runs.
- Whether there is a VTS function at all, or whether "traffic monitoring" is
  the officer watching the VHF and AIS.
- Hours: day work, watches, on-call? The posting says nothing about shifts.
- Team size: how many officers, who covers nights and leave.
- How often "deputise for the Harbour Master" actually happens.
- Company location/country is given only as "Saltmere"; the posting has no URL.

## 3. STAR stories

### A. Guiding the lifeboat to a stranded kayaker at night

*Facts: exp-skerra.f08 (verified), exp-skerra.f03 (verified).*

- **Situation.** 7 October 2023, at night: a kayaker stranded below the north
  cliff at Skerra Head.
- **Task.** Get the lifeboat to the casualty.
- **Action.** Guided the lifeboat in by VHF and searchlight from the station.
- **Result.** Casualty recovered without injury. The logbook records 26
  minutes for the lifeboat alongside — **check in the logbook what that 26
  minutes is measured from before you quote it**; if unsure, leave the number
  out.
- **Scale.** One of 14 incidents coordinated with the coastguard by VHF since
  2019, three of them helicopter medical evacuations.

Why it lands: the posting makes the officer "the port's first point of contact
for the coastguard and other emergency services". This is that job.

### B. Running the station's supply landings and budget

*Facts: exp-skerra.f05 (verified), exp-skerra.f07 (verified).*

- **Situation.** A remote station supplied by sea, four landings a year.
- **Task.** Make sure everything the station needs arrives, within budget.
- **Action.** Ran the inventory, placed the orders and agreed each landing
  plan with the supply vessel's master.
- **Result.** Four landings a year planned end to end; a consumables and
  spares budget of **about £40,000 a year** — say "about", it is an estimate.

Why it lands: "stores, fuel supplies and contractor visits" in the posting, and
the closest thing in the profile to scheduling a vessel against a plan.

### C. Keeping a first-order light in service

*Facts: exp-skerra.f01 (verified), exp-skerra.f02 (verified), exp-skerra.f10
(**draft — not yet verified**).*

- **Situation.** Sole keeper of a first-order rotating light and its fog signal,
  on a rota with one relief keeper.
- **Task.** Keep the light working.
- **Action.** Maintained the lens drive, the lamp changer and the standby
  generator.
- **Result.** Six years without an unplanned outage of the light (2018–2024).
- **Follow-on (not yet verified).** On 11 February 2025, in a storm, a drive
  belt failed; the light was restored within 40 minutes using the spare belt.
  This fact is still `draft` — verify it against the logbook with
  `cvac fact verify` before you use it, or leave it out.

Why it lands: "keep the port's aids to navigation in good order" — the posting's
nice-to-have, done as the main job.

### D. Building the logger the Lights Board plans from

*Facts: prj-fogsignal.f01 (verified), prj-fogsignal.f02 (verified).*

- **Situation.** Fog-signal activations and generator running hours were not
  being recorded in a way anyone could plan from.
- **Task.** A personal project, started in 2022.
- **Action.** Built a telemetry logger on a Raspberry Pi, written in Python.
- **Result.** Three years of continuous data; since 2023 the Lights Board's
  engineering office uses its monthly export to plan maintenance visits.

The "Situation" line is an inference from the project existing — if it was
started for another reason, say that reason instead.

Why it lands: answers the monthly-report responsibility, and is the best
evidence you will pick up their port management system quickly.

### E. Writing the station handbook and training relief keepers

*Facts: exp-skerra.f06 (verified).*

- **Situation.** Relief keepers cover the station when you are off.
- **Task.** Make sure they can run it the same way.
- **Action.** Wrote the 40-page station handbook and trained them on it.
- **Result.** Five relief keepers trained; they work from the handbook.

Why it lands: "strong written reporting skills" is a must.

**Not to use:** exp-skerra.f09 (diesel reduced "about a fifth") is draft and
the figure was never measured — do not quote it. exp-fairhaven.f05 (crossing
times −12%) is rejected.

## 4. Gap scripts

### 4.1 "Working knowledge of a port management system (berth planning, vessel scheduling)" — must

> "I haven't used a port management system. The planning I've done has been by
> hand: four supply landings a year, with the inventory, the orders and the
> landing plan agreed with the vessel's master. I also built the logger whose
> monthly export the Lights Board plans its maintenance from, so I'm
> comfortable with data and with software I have to learn myself. I'd want to
> sit with whoever runs the berth plan for the first weeks and learn the
> system on real days — which system do you use?"

Facts behind it: exp-skerra.f05, prj-fogsignal.f01, prj-fogsignal.f02. Do not
claim you have used berth-planning software; do not name a product you haven't
touched.

### 4.2 "VTS operator certification" — nice

> "I don't hold a VTS certificate. What I have is the radio side: the GMDSS
> General Operator's Certificate since 2014, and fourteen incidents worked with
> the coastguard on VHF since 2019. If the role needs the VTS qualification I'd
> take it — is that something you train for?"

Facts: exp-fairhaven.f03, exp-skerra.f03.

### 4.3 "Experience with vessel traffic monitoring" — nice

> "I haven't monitored traffic as an operator. I've been on the other side of
> the radio — on a ferry bridge making six crossings a day — so I know what a
> master needs to hear from the shore. Monitoring would be new."

Facts: exp-fairhaven.f01, exp-fairhaven.f03.

## 5. Questions to ask them

1. Which port management system do you use, and how is berth planning split
   between the system and the officers?
2. How many vessel movements does the port handle on a typical day, and on
   the busiest?
3. What does the monthly operations report cover, and who reads it?
4. How are hours organised — day work, watches, on-call? Who covers nights?
5. How many operations officers are there, and how does cover work for leave?
6. How often does the officer deputise for the Harbour Master, and for how long?
7. Who maintains the aids to navigation — what is the split between the
   contractor and the officer?
8. How much of the Brumeval link is handled in French?
9. What would a good first six months look like?

## 6. Logistics and red flags to settle

- **Compensation.** £36,000–£40,000 a year. Your floor is £34,000: the whole
  band clears it. No need to negotiate from the floor; aim for the upper half.
- **Hours.** The posting is silent. Your red flag is **rotating night shifts
  without a relief plan** — ask question 4 directly and do not accept a vague
  answer.
- **Mode and location.** Onsite in Saltmere. The posting does not give a
  region or country. Your markets are the Northern Isles (onsite), the UK coast
  (onsite/hybrid, relocation if worth moving for) and Brittany. If Saltmere is
  on the UK coast, this is a relocation: ask about start date and any
  relocation support.
- **Language.** The job is in English; French is a nice-to-have for the
  Brumeval ferry link. Be ready for a few questions in French — B2, a season on
  that route in 2017.
- **Scope.** "Deputise for the Harbour Master" can mean anything from a day's
  cover to running the port. Pin it down (question 6).
- **Confidentiality — settle before the interview.** The posting asks for a
  reference from the current employer with the application. Your search is
  confidential and the island is small. Ask whether the reference can be
  taken only after a conditional offer, and name a former referee (Marrow
  Sound Ferries) for the earlier stage if they accept one.
- **Certificates.** Confirm the GMDSS GOC and STCW basic safety are in date;
  both are on the CV and they will ask.
