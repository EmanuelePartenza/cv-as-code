# Profile questionnaire

**How to fill it in**: write your answer under each question, in your own
language, as plainly as you like. Partial answers are fine; *"I don't know"*
is a valid answer and better than a guess. Numbers only if they are real.
When you are done, change `status: to-fill` to `status: filled` in the header
and say so. Nothing you write here goes on a CV before you confirm it, fact
by fact.

## A — Who you are, for the CV header

- A1. Full name as it should appear on a CV.
- A2. City and country you live in (the CV shows the city).
- A3. E-mail address for applications.
- A4. Phone number, if it should appear at all.
- A5. Links worth showing (a professional profile, a repository, a portfolio): label and address.
- A6. Date of birth — only if the markets you apply in expect it; otherwise leave empty.

## B — Languages

- B1. For each language you can work in: the language, your level (native, or a CEFR level such as B2), and where you actually used it professionally, if anywhere.

## C — Each position you have held

Answer this block once per position, most recent first.

- C1. Role title as it was called; organisation; where (city); from when to when (month and year; "ongoing" if current).
- C2. What the organisation does, in one line.
- C3. **What you actually did**: one line per thing, as concrete as you can. Not "responsible for reporting" but "wrote the monthly reconciliation report for three business units". Include what you decided yourself, what you built, what you fixed, what you were the only one doing.
- C4. **Numbers, where they are real**: volumes, sizes, durations, counts, money, frequency, people. For each number, is it exact or an estimate?
- C5. **Results**: what changed because of your work; what would not have happened without you.
- C6. Anything you would rather not show on a CV about this position, and why.
- C7. Documents that exist about this position (reviews, appraisals, reports, tickets, code, certificates) that you could drop into `inbox/` for extraction.

## D — Projects outside a position

Answer once per project (personal, open source, volunteer, academic).

- D1. Name; what it is; from when to when; alone or with whom.
- D2. What you built or did, one line per thing; the stack or tools.
- D3. Is it used by anyone? By whom, since when, for what?
- D4. Where is it (a repository, a page)? Public or private?

## E — Education and certifications

- E1. For each degree or diploma: the title, the institution, the city, the years; anything notable (thesis, honours, a project).
- E2. Certifications and licences that matter for your target roles: name, issuer, year, still valid?

## F — Skills, with evidence

- F1. List the skills (technologies, methods, tools, domains) you would want on a CV. For each, name the position or project where you actually used it, so the skill can point at a fact.
- F2. Skills you have from study only, never used at work: list them separately.

## G — What you are looking for

- G1. Target roles, by title, in order of preference.
- G2. Roles you would also consider (adjacent).
- G3. Where: cities and countries, and for each whether on site, hybrid or remote is acceptable.
- G4. Languages a CV may be written in.
- G5. Salary floor (currency, per year or month) — the number below which you would not move.
- G6. Red flags: anything that would make you decline (shift patterns, travel, sectors, contract types).
- G7. Is your search confidential with respect to a current employer?

## H — Anything else

- H1. Anything that matters for a CV or an application and was not asked above.
